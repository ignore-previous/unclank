# D04 illustration-image-gen

Image-generator styles and artefacts that make an illustration, key art, thumbnail or marketing image read as AI: house looks, colour casts, skin, lighting, text, hands, small-object breakage, composition defaults, character drift and style imitation. Visual domain, so each tell has a Fixable line. These tells drift fast as models improve, so read the Dated line before relying on one. Most "how to spot AI images" material comes from detector or image-tool vendors and is marked (vendor). Round 2 added non-vendor sources (a photographer's blog, photography press, a university magazine, a peer-reviewed study of illustrators, an arXiv hand-quality abstract). Round 3 added a philosophy-of-aesthetics paper, press with an expert comment, a society press release on eye reflections, a loop study and a preprint that measure how outputs converge, and a user study of fallback "default images". None of these measures any single visual tell on a plain default prompt, so no tell here rests on a measured per-model figure. Rounds merged so far: R1 breadth (D04-r1-breadth), R2 depth (D04-r2-depth) and R3 depth (D04-r3-depth). Round 2 also found that several round-1 sources did not say what round 1 cited them for; those lines and tells are corrected below.

## Checklist
- [Avoid · strong] Keep generated text short and in quotes; set dense, small or background text (signs, labels, copy) in a layout tool and zoom in on every secondary text before publishing (D04-006)
- [Avoid · strong] Keep hands simple (one clear hand, or out of frame) and inpaint held objects, groups and crossed hands (D04-007)
- [Avoid · strong] Add real skin texture (pores, uneven tone, grain) and do not leave airbrushed, wax-like skin (D04-004)
- [Choose deliberately · strong] Write a style brief (medium, palette, composition, light) instead of letting a vague prompt return the model's averaged default (D04-012)
- [Choose deliberately · strong] Do not leave the generator's glossy, saturated, cinematic house look on default; set stylise or raw options and name a real medium or camera (D04-001)
- [Choose deliberately · moderate] State white balance and lighting so GPT-style output does not default to a yellow or sepia cast (D04-002)
- [Avoid · moderate] Check paired items (eyes and catchlights, ears, earrings, armour), chains, gems, repeated objects and textures, background items and perspective on a second look, and simplify or inpaint what breaks (D04-009)
- [Avoid · moderate] Use a reference image or character sheet for any series, so one character stays one character (D04-011)
- [Avoid · moderate] Do not imitate a named studio's or living artist's style (the 2025 Ghibli-style wave); develop your own look (D04-003)

## Tells

### D04-001 Glossy over-rendered house look (a generator default, not one tool)
- Label: Choose deliberately
- Confidence: strong (changed from moderate in round 3, on count, merger's call; lead to confirm. Fetched non-vendor origins that describe a polished, saturated, high-contrast default finish: a 2024 forum thread (S-D04-004), a 2026 journal essay (S-D04-052) and a 2024 press piece with an expert comment (S-D04-053); a 2023 photography-press test (S-D04-051) adds partial support on skin. Other sources name different tools and none measures the look.) Lead review 20 Oct (r2): held at moderate. The sources name different tools and none measures anything. Lead review 20 Oct (r3): strong accepted. Three independent, fetched, non-vendor origins: the HN thread, Declos in JAAC, and The Atlantic. None measures the look, and Declos found it partly tool-specific, so name the tool when you apply this check.
- Dated: noted from about 2023-2024 (a photography-press test of 22 Dec 2023, S-D04-051; forum thread of 16 Aug 2024, S-D04-004; press piece of 16 Aug 2024, S-D04-053), still cited in 2026 (journal essay of 23 Apr 2026, S-D04-052). Round 1 said some 2026 material reports that the newest versions reduced the look; round 2 found no fetched page that says so, so that claim is dropped. The fetched 2026 pages go the other way: one review calls the look a "signature aesthetic" and a strength (S-D04-001), a September 2026 review credits Midjourney's colour harmony and strong realism (S-D04-038), and a February 2026 comparison credits Midjourney with gritty realism (S-D04-005). A search-only vendor claim that Midjourney V7 largely lost its airbrushed look has no named source here. So the look is drifting, with no evidence either way that it is gone.
- Why it reads as AI: A polished render finish (smooth skin, cinematic glow, high contrast, a background that melts into generic bokeh) arrives whether or not the prompt asked for it. A 2026 essay in a philosophy journal argues by example that image generators lean toward exaggerated vibrancy, bright light and sharp contrast (S-D04-052). A 2024 press piece quotes an expert saying most models run an aesthetic filter on input and output (S-D04-053); the list of visual traits there is the reporter's, not the expert's. A competitor's page says experienced viewers can name the platform at a glance (S-D04-002); that is marketing, not a test. Forum commenters tie the gloss to training preference and post-processing (S-D04-004), which is opinion.
- Fix: Lower the stylise value or use the raw style setting so the prompt wins over the house look. Name a real film stock, camera or medium instead of "8K" or "Unreal Engine". Ask for texture and imperfection. Check each tool's default before choosing one.
- Fixable: partly. Settings and prompting reduce it; a model's default aesthetic does not vanish.
- Sources: S-D04-001, S-D04-002, S-D04-003, S-D04-004, S-D04-005, S-D04-038, S-D04-051, S-D04-052, S-D04-053
- Flag: Moved to strong on count only; lead to confirm. The three origins counted are all argument or opinion, none measures the look. The essay's author (S-D04-052) says tests on other tools gave less striking results and that empirical confirmation is still needed, so the look is tool-specific as much as general. The Atlantic piece reached via Berkeley (S-D04-053) is cited for the filter point only; the expert's comment is judgement with no test. S-D04-051 is promotional and covers 2023 models (SDXL and Midjourney of that era). Round 1 attached this tell to Midjourney. The fetched sources split: S-D04-005 puts the glossy "easily recognizable AI look" on OpenArt and says Midjourney has gritty realism; S-D04-001 treats Midjourney's look as a strength and does not say it has reduced; S-D04-038 is counter-evidence. The tell is rescoped to an unprompted glossy default in any generator. S-D04-002 is a competitor's blog (vendor). S-D04-003 is search only and of unconfirmed date. S-D04-004 is a forum question, opinion only. A February 2026 "Midjourney Glaze" guide and a named review saying the look is gone were searched for in round 2 and not found; neither is recorded. Overlaps D04-012 (generic default style).

### D04-002 Yellow or sepia cast on GPT-style image output
- Label: Choose deliberately
- Confidence: moderate (3 sources: one fetched article, a Japanese explainer and a meme page; the last two search only)
- Dated: noted from 2025 after the GPT-4o image launch (launch date from a search summary, not a source opened); still discussed in March 2026 (S-D04-006). No confirmed fix from OpenAI. Freepik claimed in May 2025 that an update removed unwanted yellow tints (S-D04-009); that is a vendor claim with no independent check, and the March 2026 article implies the cast persists. Whether the newest GPT image models still have it was not tested.
- Why it reads as AI: With no lighting or white balance specified, output leans warm, so many unrelated images share an old-photo or filtered wash. It is common enough to be a meme.
- Fix: State white balance and light in the prompt (neutral white, daylight, "no warm cast") and avoid words such as vintage, parchment or golden hour. Otherwise correct colour afterwards with white balance, curves or LAB. Regenerating usually does not help.
- Fixable: yes by prompting and editing.
- Sources: S-D04-006, S-D04-007, S-D04-008, S-D04-009
- Flag: Only S-D04-006 was fetched, and it says OpenAI has published no root cause, so its list of causes is speculation. S-D04-007 to S-D04-009 are search snippets only; S-D04-009 is vendor-sourced. Round 2 looked for a 2026 source on the newest GPT image models and found none either way.

### D04-003 Imitating a named studio's style (the Ghibli-style wave)
- Label: Avoid
- Confidence: moderate (6 sources: 4 on the trend and backlash, 1 peer-reviewed study of illustrators, 1 press statement; only the study bears on how the look reads, and it covers fine-tuned copies, not prompted styles)
- Dated: March-April 2025 (OpenAI image launch and backlash), still cited in 2025-2026 discussion. The CHI 2025 study (arXiv v2, 30 Jan 2025) is the first artist-level visual evidence. Not clear the tell is still active; a change of model does not fix a provenance problem.
- Why it reads as AI: Copying a recognisable studio style from a prompt makes the output look generic and mass-produced, and the trend tied that look to low-effort generation. In the CHI 2025 study four illustrators judged fine-tuned copies of their own styles; copies carried fragments of colour, texture and shading but not the choices that make the style, and one illustrator called the colour simplification "very inelegant" (S-D04-042). The objection from artists is mainly uncredited imitation of hand-drawn craft and lost commissions.
- Fix: Develop your own look and a reason for it. If a reference is needed, use your own or licensed material and say so.
- Fixable: partly. Prompting can move away from the copied look, but the consent and provenance issue is not fixed by editing the image.
- Sources: S-D04-010, S-D04-011, S-D04-012, S-D04-013, S-D04-042, S-D04-043
- Flag: This is mostly an ethics and provenance tell, not a visual one; no artist or critic found calls the prompted look itself a visual tell. S-D04-042 studies fine-tuned copies of four illustrators, not default prompts, and its artists judged other illustrators' copies as more successful than their own, so it does not show one-way failure. S-D04-012, S-D04-013 and S-D04-043 are search snippets only; S-D04-043 (Karla Ortiz on the 2025 filter) is an ethics statement, not visual evidence. S-D04-011 is a thread title and score only. Do not cite Miyazaki as reacting to the 2025 trend: the fetched S-D04-010 quotes only a 2016 reaction to a different AI animation demo. A Julien Posture post dated 29 Sep 2024 summarises S-D04-042, so it is not an independent source and is not added.

### D04-004 Plastic or airbrushed skin
- Label: Avoid
- Confidence: strong (changed from moderate in round 2; 3 independent sources that directly describe over-smooth skin: a photographer's blog and a photography-press piece, both fetched non-vendor, plus an API reseller comparison; a vendor guide, a blog comparison and a forum thread add partial support) Lead review 20 Oct (r2): strong accepted. A photographer and Fstoppers (both fetched, non-vendor) plus one reseller make three origins. On currency, Optic Flux (Jul 2026) says skin smoothing is largely solved in newer models, but it gives no test, model or data (checked in round 3), so its line counts as no evidence either way, not as a counter-source. The 2026 sources that do compare models still split, so check the current model before relying on this tell.
- Dated: noted from about 2023, still cited in 2026, but 2026 sources split. A 24 Apr 2026 reseller comparison says GPT Image 2 skin is "slightly plastic" and Nano Banana Pro skin has pore detail (S-D04-015). A 22 Apr 2026 blog test says GPT Image 2 selfies come out "too clean, too evenly lit" and that Nano Banana Pro wins on skin (S-D04-016); it does not use the word plastic. A 30 Jul 2026 guide says skin smoothness is "solved by most current-generation models" (S-D04-029), with no test behind it. A 4 Sep 2026 vendor test (getimg.ai, S-D04-061) rates two current models as the "most convincing balance"; it is unblinded, unrepeated and unscored, so it is vendor evidence only. No independent 2026 test of skin smoothing in current commercial models was found. Read it as widely documented and improving, not fixed everywhere. None of this is independently measured.
- Why it reads as AI: Models trained on retouched or compressed photos treat smooth, pore-free skin as the default, so faces read as wax figures or heavy beauty filters. A photographer describes AI removing skin texture until the face looks uncanny (S-D04-017), and a photography-press piece says viewers assume overly smooth work is fake (S-D04-039).
- Fix: Describe real skin texture (pores, fine lines, uneven tone), add film grain, and keep sharpening low. Over-sharpening every pore also looks synthetic.
- Fixable: yes by prompting and editing.
- Sources: S-D04-014, S-D04-015, S-D04-016, S-D04-017, S-D04-039, S-D04-004, S-D04-029, S-D04-061
- Flag: Moved to strong on count; lead to confirm. S-D04-017 and S-D04-039 are the fetched non-vendor sources. S-D04-039 (Fstoppers) promotes a paid course and its smoothness line is about work in general, not generators alone. S-D04-015 is an API reseller (vendor) with no test described. S-D04-014 is a vendor that sells the prompt pack behind its tests. S-D04-016 does not say "plastic" (round 1 cited it for that; corrected above) and ran ten prompts once per model. S-D04-004 is a thread about gloss in general. S-D04-029 is an unsupported assertion on currency (no test), so it is weighed as no evidence either way. S-D04-061 is vendor evidence and its source line has no URL because the raw file did not record one. Round 1's claim that a photographer describes "an evenness no real skin has" came from a search summary and is not in the fetched text; dropped.

### D04-005 Uniform glowing halo or rim around the subject
- Label: Avoid
- Confidence: anecdotal (2 prompt-tool vendor guides, both search only)
- Dated: undated; looks current in 2026 prompt-trend material. No source says newer models fixed it.
- Why it reads as AI: A bright edge of even thickness drawn around the subject, not tied to a light source in the scene, reads as pasted-on glow. The same bright edge against a bright background becomes a halo.
- Fix: Put a real light source in the scene and let a thin rim follow it from one side. Check the background for a halo.
- Fixable: yes by prompting and editing.
- Sources: S-D04-018, S-D04-019
- Flag: Anecdotal. Both sources are vendor prompt guides, search snippets only, and neither is an illustrator or critic. Rim light as a technique is recorded under D04-N03.

### D04-006 Garbled or pseudo text in the image
- Label: Avoid
- Confidence: strong (changed from moderate in round 2; scoped to dense, small and background text. Fetched sources: a university-magazine test, two guides, two reviews or blogs, two of them vendors) Lead review 20 Oct (r2): strong accepted for the scoped claim only (dense, small, background or non-Latin text). Clean foreground headline text is no longer a reliable tell.
- Dated: widely cited from 2023-2024. An art-press test in fall 2023 found nonsense letters in a DALL-E image (S-D04-040). Still a flaw in a November 2025 review (S-D04-020: strong text rendering, but too many words by default and typos remain). In 2026 the sources agree on the split: short English text mostly works (S-D04-021, September 2026), garbled foreground signage is mostly resolved, but storefront, licence-plate and book-spine text still fails (S-D04-029, 30 Jul 2026), and longer passages, small, curved and repeated text still fail (S-D04-028, September 2026). Non-Latin scripts still fail (S-D04-021). So the tell is weak for short prominent English text and live for dense, small, background and non-Latin text.
- Why it reads as AI: Models treat letters partly as texture, so shop signs, labels, logos and book covers drift or dissolve even when the scene is right. At a distance the text looks like text, so the failure shows on close inspection.
- Fix: Keep text short and in quotes, use a model known for text, repair with region edits, or add the real type in a layout tool after generation. Do not use generated text for dense copy, tables or non-Latin scripts. Zoom in on every secondary text before publishing.
- Fixable: partly. Short English text is mostly reliable on current models; dense, small, background and non-Latin text still needs a layout tool.
- Sources: S-D04-020, S-D04-021, S-D04-022, S-D04-023, S-D04-040, S-D04-029, S-D04-028, S-D04-017, S-D04-047
- Flag: "Short text now mostly works" rests on S-D04-021 (vendor) and S-D04-029 (unsigned guide), with no independent test. A reseller comparison (S-D04-015) says GPT Image 2 placed labels precisely, from one run per model. S-D04-028 is a vendor. S-D04-022 is a search snippet only (its page returned 404). The Clutch survey figures that tied to it are traced to the press release S-D04-047: 66% confident before testing, 57% wrong when tested, with no sample size, fieldwork dates or test design, from a services directory. They concern people's ability to spot AI images, not this tell, and are not used to carry any confidence. S-D04-023 is a search snippet only and supports the detector side (detectors are weaker on text-rich fakes), not the visual tell. The most direct study of long and dense text (STRICT, arXiv 2505.18985) could not be read, and search summaries of it conflict, so no figures are used.

### D04-007 Hands: groups, held objects and crossed hands
- Label: Avoid
- Confidence: strong (changed from moderate in round 2; for complex cases only. Fetched sources: a November 2025 review, a July 2026 guide, a photographer's blog, an arXiv abstract and a September 2026 vendor post; two more are search only) Lead review 20 Oct (r2): strong accepted for complex poses only (gripping, overlapping or partly hidden hands). A plain open hand is no longer a reliable tell.
- Dated: the extra-finger tell was widely cited in 2023-2024; failures moved to harder compositions in 2025. A September 2026 vendor post calls a single clear hand close to a non-issue and says the old claim that AI cannot draw hands is outdated, while interlocking hands, gripped objects and small hands in crowds still fail (S-D04-021). A 30 Jul 2026 guide agrees: resting hands are fine, gripping, overlapping or partly hidden hands still get extra or fused fingers "more often than not" (S-D04-029). A November 2025 review still saw one figure with three hands (S-D04-020). An October 2025 arXiv abstract says hand quality still shows structural distortions (S-D04-041). Treat single clear hands as largely fixed; complex ones as live.
- Why it reads as AI: Hands have no fixed shape, they occlude themselves and objects, and training data for them is scarce, so fingers fuse into cups, hilts and phone cases or multiply in crowded scenes. A photographer also lists wrong joints and mismatched pairs (S-D04-017).
- Fix: Compose hands simply (one clear hand, or out of frame). Check grips on held objects, and inpaint a small mask with a precise prompt such as five separate fingers.
- Fixable: partly. Simple poses are reliable; groups and grips still need composition choices or inpainting.
- Sources: S-D04-020, S-D04-021, S-D04-024, S-D04-025, S-D04-041, S-D04-029, S-D04-017
- Flag: No independent per-model benchmark with measured error rates for 2026 models was found: S-D04-041 is an abstract only (the PDF was unreadable) with no per-model numbers, and the "mostly fixed" claims are vendor or unsigned-guide statements. S-D04-024 and S-D04-025 are search snippets only. Label is Avoid for the complex cases; a single clear hand is not a tell. The strong rating applies to the complex-case scope only.

### D04-008 Symmetric, centred, front-lit, eye-level default composition
- Label: Choose deliberately
- Confidence: anecdotal (1 vendor guide, fetched, plus 1 uncredited opinion piece of unknown provenance that is search-only grade; round 3 resolved that the aiarthive blog's "symmetry" is not composition, so it no longer counts)
- Dated: 27 May 2026 guide (S-D04-014); the uncredited piece (S-D04-048) shows only "2 years ago". No source says whether newer models fixed it.
- Why it reads as AI: When the prompt is vague, output falls back to a centred, mirror-symmetric layout with flat front light and a plain grey or white backdrop, where photos and art usually use off-centre framing and directional light. The vendor guide says this resembles the average of stock headshots. The uncredited piece adds that the camera is almost always head-on at eye level, with no high, low or tilted angle, and says the models cannot reliably produce other angles; that is one unattributed opinion.
- Fix: Specify framing (subject share of frame, off-centre, thirds), a camera height and angle (for example low three-quarter, 35mm), a light direction, an asymmetric pose and a named backdrop or room. Keep symmetry or a frontal shot on purpose where the design needs it, such as an emblem, a poster grid, a portrait or a product shot.
- Fixable: yes by prompting and editing.
- Sources: S-D04-014, S-D04-048
- Flag: S-D04-014 (Lifehacked AI, fetched in round 2) is a vendor that sells the prompt pack behind its tests; its before-and-after results are its own, and it says its tactics only lower failure rates. S-D04-048 is an uncredited Vocal Media piece (byline "General gyan", dated only "2 years ago"); it never names Keith Kisser, so it is not a read of S-D04-030 and is not attributed to him. It is counted as search-only grade, and it may be a copy of another essay, so it is not an independent origin. Corrections applied in round 3: S-D04-035 (aiarthive) lists "symmetry" as mismatched left and right items, not centred layouts, so it is removed here and used in D04-009 and D04-015; S-D04-026 (the 2023 Psychological Science paper) is about averageness driving human judgements of AI faces, not symmetry, and is removed from this tell. A frontal, eye-level shot is a standard choice, so the tell is the default with no reason. See D04-N05.

### D04-009 Small details that fail on a second look
- Label: Avoid
- Confidence: moderate (round 2 opened all three round-1 vendor sources and added a fetched photographer's blog; round 3 added non-vendor sources for two sub-claims. Eye-catchlight mismatch: a photographer's blog, a 2024 society press release and a 2020 paper abstract. Mismatched left-right pairs and repeated textures: one blog. Chains and gems: retouching vendors only.)
- Dated: 2025-2026 guides (Jewels Retouch 3 Apr 2026, Endertech 5 Sep 2026, photographer's blog 2026, aiarthive 2 Jun 2025); a society press release of 17 Jul 2024 (S-D04-049) and a 2020 paper (S-D04-050) cover the eye reflections of GAN-era or deepfake faces, not current diffusion models. No source says newer models fixed it. The jewellery claims come from retouching vendors with a commercial interest.
- Why it reads as AI: The main object is right but small objects get weaker geometry. Things that should match across the body do not: eyes with disagreeing catchlights or reflections, ears at different heights, armour pieces or jewellery with different designs on each side. Textures repeat: identical clouds, identical fabric folds, rows of bricks, tiles, windows or chairs. Hair and other complex edges drift toward an average, chain links and prongs melt, gems go flat, gold reads as painted plastic, and perspective or shadows conflict. The eye case is explained by an imaging paper as models lacking the physical constraint that links both eyes to one light source (S-D04-050).
- Fix: Simplify the scene and cut repeated small objects. Check every paired item (eyes, ears, earrings, armour, jewellery) at full zoom, ask for matching pairs by name (two identical hoop earrings), and inpaint the odd side to match. Check catchlights, reflections and perspective, retouch or remove broken jewellery, and inpaint the region before publishing.
- Fixable: partly. Fewer small objects and inpainting help; hard scenes still break.
- Sources: S-D04-027, S-D04-028, S-D04-029, S-D04-017, S-D04-035, S-D04-049, S-D04-050
- Flag: The catchlight sub-claim alone has three fetched non-vendor origins (S-D04-017, S-D04-049, S-D04-050), but two of them concern GAN-era or deepfake faces, the press release (S-D04-049) reports no accuracy figures and names no generators, and the abstract (S-D04-050) gives none; the tell as a whole mixes sub-claims, and chains and gems are vendor-only. So I kept moderate; lead may split the pairs-and-catchlights sub-claim into its own tell. S-D04-035 (aiarthive, blog) is the only source for mismatched pairs and for repeated clouds and fabric folds; its section on this covers eyes, ears, armour and jewellery. Corrected in round 2: round 1 said earrings fuse into skin, but the fetched S-D04-027 covers chains, prongs, gems and metal and never mentions earrings; that claim is dropped (earrings now appear only as an example of a paired item, from the raw file's reading of S-D04-035). S-D04-027 and S-D04-028 are vendors. S-D04-028 lists earrings, eyeglasses and teeth as small objects to zoom into, which is advice to check, not a described failure. S-D04-029 was opened in round 2 but the raw file does not confirm it for this claim, so treat it as unconfirmed. The guides concede that human artists and ordinary editing cause similar errors, so no single detail proves AI. Irregular catchlight shapes are not the tell (D04-N06). The shadow and light-source part overlaps D04-015.

### D04-010 Uniform detail with no focal hierarchy
- Label: Avoid
- Confidence: anecdotal (lead review 20 Oct: downgraded from moderate; the one artist essay could not be opened (403) and the forum thread makes a related, not the same, claim). Round 2: the essay returned 403 again. A peer-reviewed study of illustrators (S-D04-042) adds related evidence but studies fine-tuned style copies, not default prompts, so the level is left unchanged. Round 3 (lead, 20 Oct): the essay returned 403 a third time; a Vocal Media page with the same title (S-D04-048) is uncredited and of unknown provenance, so it does not reverse the downgrade and is not attributed to the essay's author.
- Dated: an artist essay of about 2023 (date unverified), the CHI 2025 study (arXiv v2, 30 Jan 2025; fieldwork 2024), stock-site rejection threads from December 2025 to February 2026, and a 2026 guide. The uncredited Vocal page shows only "2 years ago". No source says newer models fixed it.
- Why it reads as AI: Every area gets similar sharpness and detail, so there is no clear subject, no layered depth and no foreshortening; the background is either smooth or busy. One illustrator in the CHI study put it as texture applied regardless of what it depicts, asking "Is it grass? Or is it rocks?" (S-D04-042), and described fragments of detail that come out right but are never assembled into a whole. The uncredited piece makes a similar point about hair, skin and fabric taking one moulded texture (S-D04-048).
- Fix: Decide what matters. Give the subject the most detail and contrast, push the background back with less detail, a value shift or haze, use negative space, and describe depth planes in the prompt or draw them in.
- Fixable: yes by prompting and editing.
- Sources: S-D04-030, S-D04-031, S-D04-029, S-D04-042, S-D04-048
- Flag: S-D04-030, the one attributed artist essay, is a search snippet only: the page returned 403 in rounds 1, 2 and 3 and its date is unverified. S-D04-048 is an uncredited opinion piece with the same title, byline "General gyan", dated only "2 years ago", and never mentions Keith Kisser; its provenance is unknown, so it is search-only grade and not a read of S-D04-030. S-D04-031 is a stock-contributor forum thread about rejections for noise, over-smoothing and artefacts, which is related but not the same claim. S-D04-042 is a fetched peer-reviewed study with four illustrators, but about fine-tuned copies of their own styles. A Julien Posture post dated 29 Sep 2024 summarises S-D04-042, so it is not an independent source and is not added. If the lead accepts S-D04-042 as an artist voice, moderate (2 sources) would follow; left at anecdotal because both usable sources are "related, not the same". Detailed, busy backgrounds are a valid style in hand-made fantasy art. S-D04-029 was not re-checked for this tell in round 3.

### D04-011 Character drift across a set
- Label: Avoid
- Confidence: moderate (round 2: 2 fetched sources support drift, a 2023 test by a named UX researcher and a September 2026 vendor post; a third, vendor page claims stable features, so it partly counters)
- Dated: A 15 Dec 2023 test found that a Midjourney-made avatar used as reference still gave a hair-colour shift, and the author called it "still too challenging" to keep the same character (S-D04-033); that predates today's reference tools. A September 2026 post says a reference image is needed and that consistency is a vendor-listed limitation (S-D04-021). A 2026 vendor page claims face shape, hair, skin tone and style "stay stable" and admits "tiny differences" (S-D04-032). Reference tools have improved; drift is described as reduced, not gone. Round 1 said this vendor's test showed eye shape and skin tone shifting; the fetched page does not say that, so the claim is dropped.
- Why it reads as AI: With no fixed identity, each generation resamples a character from the whole training distribution, so features such as hair colour change between scenes. This breaks comics, children's books and brand mascots.
- Fix: Use a canonical reference image or character sheet on every generation, describe features in the same words each time, review the set side by side, and accept some drift for a series.
- Fixable: partly. References and sheets reduce drift but do not eliminate it.
- Sources: S-D04-021, S-D04-032, S-D04-033
- Flag: Currency rests on one vendor post (S-D04-021); the independent test (S-D04-033) is from 2023. ### D04-012 Unprompted model default aesthetic
- Label: Choose deliberately
- Confidence: strong (changed from moderate in round 2; 3 fetched non-vendor sources: a November 2025 review, a photographer's blog and a February 2026 tool comparison, plus competitor vendor pages counted as one. Round 3 added fetched non-vendor sources on convergence: a peer-reviewed loop study, a July 2026 preprint, a 2026 journal essay and a TechRadar piece.) Lead review 20 Oct (r2): strong accepted. It rests on three fetched non-vendor sources, all opinion or review with no measurement.
- Dated: 2025-2026. A November 2025 review says Nano Banana Pro falls back on a generic AI-image look without detailed prompting, and that the "AI cartooning" default belongs to an earlier ChatGPT image model (S-D04-020). A photographer says models lean toward vivid, high-contrast output learned from feed-optimised material (S-D04-017). A February 2026 comparison says Midjourney's default "wants to make things look epic" (S-D04-005). A competitor's blog says Midjourney portraits show smoothed skin and slightly over-saturated eyes (S-D04-002), and a 2026 vendor review says Nano Banana Pro defaults to saturated, high-contrast, slightly digital-illustration output (S-D04-034). A 2026 journal essay argues generators carry an aesthetic bias toward vibrancy and contrast (S-D04-052). A 2025 study put Stable Diffusion XL and a captioning model in feedback loops (700 runs of 100 cycles) and found the images converge on about 12 generic scene types, such as lighthouses, gothic and palatial interiors and dramatic landscapes (S-D04-054; a PetaPixel report of 23 Dec 2025 is search only, S-D04-055). - Why it reads as AI: A vague prompt returns the model's averaged house style, with no point of view and no choices that are the creator's. Experienced viewers spot the house style across a feed. Part of the sameness comes from many people using the same tool, presets and one-shot prompts: a cartoonist and an agency founder quoted by TechRadar say posters and menus all look alike (S-D04-059), and a digital artist says to avoid presets and default prompts (S-D04-058).

Keep a short style brief per project and compare two models before picking one. Avoid presets and default prompts, pick the subject from your own project rather than a stock-like scene, and use known words, not made-up style words.
- Fixable: yes by prompting and editing.
- Sources: S-D04-020, S-D04-017, S-D04-005, S-D04-002, S-D04-034, S-D04-013, S-D04-052, S-D04-054, S-D04-055, S-D04-056, S-D04-057, S-D04-058, S-D04-059, S-D04-060

### D04-013 Smeared or invented signatures
- Label: Avoid
- Confidence: anecdotal (changed from moderate in round 2; both round-1 sources were opened and neither mentions signatures; the only remaining source is one search-only press item about a different phenomenon)
- Dated: Garbled signatures on Lensa portraits in 2022 are mentioned only in a search result with no URL recovered, so they are not counted. A 5 Oct 2026 report says ChatGPT-made New Yorker-style cartoons carry readable pen names of more than 15 real cartoonists, and that OpenAI did not explain how (S-D04-044, search summary only).
- Why it reads as AI: A signature that matches no real artist, or a real artist's name on work they did not make, signals generated work and is a provenance problem. Whether models draw blurred corner marks as texture is not supported by any source in this file.
- Fix: Ask for no signature, or remove it. Add a real one after generation, in your own hand or as a logo. Never add a fake signature, or a name you do not own, to suggest a human artist.
- Fixable: yes by prompting and editing (a stray mark); provenance is not fixed by editing.
- Sources: S-D04-044, S-D04-035, S-D04-029
- Flag: Sources don't support the claim. Round 1 cited S-D04-035 and S-D04-029 for smeared signatures; the fetched pages never mention signatures, so neither supports it. The remaining S-D04-044 is a search snippet only (page returned 403) and concerns forged real names, a provenance issue more than a visual one. Dropped from the checklist (only strong and moderate go there). Real artists also sign with unclear marks, so an unreadable mark is weak evidence. Keep Avoid either way.

### D04-014 Stock AI imagery in illustrations about AI
- Label: Choose deliberately
- Confidence: anecdotal (2 posts by one illustrator, counted as one source)

### D04-015 Lighting with no single source: multiple suns and contradicting shadows
- Label: Avoid
- Confidence: anecdotal (1 fetched blog, aiarthive, plus 1 uncredited opinion piece of unknown provenance that is search-only grade; the second may not be an independent origin)
- Dated: aiarthive 2 Jun 2025 (fetched); the uncredited piece shows only "2 years ago". No source says newer models fixed it.
- Why it reads as AI: Light in the scene seems to come from several unexplained places, shadows fall against the stated source, or glow and bloom replace direction. The model blends lighting from many training images, so the scene has no single origin of light. The uncredited piece also describes flat, overcast or hazy scenes with pools of light from random places.
- Fix: Name one light source and its direction, state a single key light, and check that shadows fall away from it on every object. Inpaint or paint out shadows that contradict it.
- Fixable: yes by prompting and editing.
- Sources: S-D04-035, S-D04-048
- Flag: Anecdotal. The aiarthive list ("multiple suns", shadows that contradict the source) is a paraphrase from the raw file's reading of the fetched page. S-D04-048 is an uncredited opinion piece (byline "General gyan", dated only "2 years ago"); it never mentions Keith Kisser, so it is not a read of S-D04-030 and not attributed to him. Real scenes have mixed light (reflected light, fill, practical lamps), so a conflict only counts when no source in the scene explains it. Flat, front-lit default light is kept under D04-008; shadow conflict also appears in D04-009.

## Not a tell

### D04-N01 Painterly brushwork and soft light, in themselves
- Why it is fine: Visible brushwork, painted texture and diffuse light are ordinary craft. A 2026 guide puts the tell in uniform, unmotivated texture (strokes that start and stop nowhere, the same paint texture on unrelated surfaces, even detail with no human simplification), not in painterly work as such. The same guide warns that artists make similar mistakes. When the texture is motivated by the scene, it passes. Conflict: the same guide also lists painted-on brushwork as a tell, and the default house look (D04-001) includes soft painterly light, so judge intent, not the effect. Anecdotal; search snippet only.
- Sources: S-D04-029

### D04-N02 Shallow depth of field and bokeh as a camera technique
- Why it is fine: A sharp subject on a soft background is standard photographic craft, and no source calls bokeh an AI tell. I put it here, not in Tells, because the only source (S-D04-037) makes a trend claim (the look has spread alongside AI tools), not a detection claim. Heavy, uniform bokeh on every image may become one; the version that dissolves a background into generic bokeh is covered inside D04-001. Anecdotal; search snippet only.
- Sources: S-D04-037

### D04-N04 Teal-and-orange grade
- Why it is fine: A standard film grade; no source calls it an AI tell. ### D04-N05 Deliberate mirror symmetry in emblems, logos and poster grids
- Why it is fine: Symmetry is a design choice for emblems, logos and grids. The one fetched blog that uses the word "symmetry" (S-D04-035) flags failed symmetry in paired items (eyes, ears, armour, jewellery), not mirrored or centred layouts. The tell is an unplanned default composition (D04-008), not symmetry as such. Anecdotal; one blog.
- Sources: S-D04-035, S-D04-014

### D04-N06 Irregular or non-round catchlights as such (strip, triangular, window shapes)
- Why it is fine: Real photographs show strip, triangular and rectangular catchlights from strip boxes and windows, and a 2023 informal test reports that lay viewers read real ones as fake. The signal is a mismatch between the two eyes or with the scene's light source (D04-009), not the shape. Counter: a reader comment in the same thread calls wonky catchlights a good tell. Anecdotal; the test is informal, promotional and unmeasured.
- Sources: S-D04-051
