# Year 7 Science: Our Solar System

Twelve single-best-answer questions for the end-of-unit quiz. The key line gives the correct option, then an explanation and the feedback shown to the learner.

## Q1

What is the Sun mostly made of?

A. Molten iron and nickel
B. Hydrogen and helium, with hydrogen the most abundant
C. Oxygen and nitrogen
D. Carbon dioxide and water vapour

**Key:** B
**Explanation:** The Sun is mostly hydrogen and helium.
**Feedback if correct:** Great job! You know what the Sun is made of.
**Feedback if incorrect:** The Sun is mostly hydrogen and helium. Review the lesson on stars.

## Q2

What causes the changing seasons on Earth?

A. The planet is always closest to the Sun in summer
B. The tilt of Earth's axis changes how directly sunlight reaches each hemisphere during the seasons
C. The Moon blocks some of the sunlight each month
D. The Sun only gives out heat in summer

**Key:** B
**Explanation:** Earth's axis is tilted about 23.5 degrees. A hemisphere tilted towards the Sun gets more direct light and longer days, so it is summer there. Many learners pick A because summer feels like being nearer the Sun, but Earth is actually closest to the Sun in early January, in the northern winter.
**Feedback if correct:** Correct. The tilt, not the distance, changes the angle of the sunlight.
**Feedback if incorrect:** Not quite. Distance from the Sun changes little over the year. Think about the tilt of the axis and the angle of the light.

## Q3

Why does the Moon show different phases?

A. Earth's shadow always covers a different part of it
B. We see different amounts of the Moon's lit half as the Moon moves around Earth
C. The Sun never lights the same side twice
D. Clouds cover only some of its surface each night

**Key:** B
**Explanation:** B is correct because we see different amounts of the Moon's lit half as it moves around Earth.
**Feedback if correct:** Well done! That is the right answer.
**Feedback if incorrect:** The correct answer is B. Read the section on the Moon again.

## Q6

Which of the following is NOT a planet in our solar system?

A. Venus
B. Titan, which orbits Saturn
C. Mars
D. Jupiter

**Key:** B
**Explanation:** Titan is not a planet. It is Saturn's largest moon.
**Feedback if correct:** Correct. Titan is a moon, not a planet.
**Feedback if incorrect:** Good try! Titan is the one that is not a planet. It is Saturn's largest moon.

## Q7

Which of these can be found in our solar system?

A. Comets
B. Asteroids
C. Moons
D. All of the above

**Key:** D
**Explanation:** Comets, asteroids and moons all belong to the solar system. Learners sometimes think only planets count, so they stop at the first option that names a familiar object. Each option names a different kind of body that orbits the Sun or a planet.
**Feedback if correct:** Correct. The solar system holds far more than the Sun and eight planets.
**Feedback if incorrect:** Check each option separately. Each one names a kind of body that is part of the solar system.

## Q8

Which force keeps the planets in orbit around the Sun?

A. Magnetism, because only the Sun's magnetic field reaches that far
B. Gravity pulling each planet towards the Sun while its sideways motion carries it onwards
C. Friction with the dust between the planets
D. Wind from the Sun pushing the planets round

**Key:** B
**Explanation:** The force that keeps planets in orbit around the Sun is gravity.
**Feedback if correct:** Great job! Gravity is the force that holds the planets in orbit.
**Feedback if incorrect:** The answer is gravity. Look again at the lesson on forces.

## Q9

About how long does Earth take to travel once around the Sun?

A. 24 hours

## Q10

Which planet is the largest in our solar system?

A. Jupiter, which has more than twice the mass of all the other planets combined
B. Saturn
C. Neptune
D. Earth

**Key:** A
**Explanation:** Jupiter is the largest planet. Saturn is the second largest and has the most famous rings, so learners often pick it, but Saturn has less than a third of Jupiter's mass. Neptune is far from the Sun but much smaller, and Earth is a small rocky planet.
**Feedback if correct:** Correct. Jupiter's mass is more than twice that of every other planet together.
**Feedback if incorrect:** Compare the sizes in the planet table. Saturn is large and well known for its rings, but Jupiter is larger.

## Q11

Which gas makes up most of Earth's atmosphere?

A. Oxygen
B. Carbon dioxide
C. Nitrogen, at about 78 percent of the air
D. Argon

**Key:** C
**Explanation:** Nitrogen makes up about 78 percent of the air and oxygen about 21 percent. Many learners pick oxygen because it is the gas we breathe and the one we talk about most, but it is the second most common gas. Carbon dioxide and argon are only traces.
**Feedback if correct:** Correct. Nitrogen is the most common gas, with oxygen second.
**Feedback if incorrect:** Oxygen is the gas we need, which makes it feel like the main one, but nitrogen is more common. Check the pie chart of the air.

## Q12

A star is described as 4 light-years away. What does that tell you?

A. The star is 4 years old
B. Its light takes 4 years to reach us, so we see the star as it was 4 years ago
C. The star would take 4 years to fly to at the speed of sound
D. The star is 4 times as bright as the Sun

**Key:** B
**Explanation:** A light-year is a distance, the distance light travels in one year, so 4 light-years means the light we see left the star about 4 years ago. The word "year" leads many learners to think it measures time, which is why A is tempting. C confuses light with sound, and D confuses distance with brightness.
**Feedback if correct:** Correct. A light-year is a unit of distance, and the light we see is 4 years old.
**Feedback if incorrect:** A light-year is a distance, not a length of time. Ask how far light travels in a year.
